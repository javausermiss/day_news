Meet the under-35s shaping the future of biotech
They’re designing viruses, reversing the aging process, and saving lives.
Every year, MIT Technology Review puts together a list of some of the brightest and best young minds working across science and technology. Our 35 Innovators Under 35 are the ones to watch—people whose research and technical work stands to shape the future of their fields.
This year, the list includes nine people who are transforming biotech. And this week, I’m going to give you a taste of some of the very cool stuff five of them are working on, which includes lifesaving innovations and groundbreaking “age reversal” tech.
1. Preventing maternal deaths
Let’s start with Paschal Kija, a 28-year-old who has developed a device to treat postpartum hemorrhage—a dangerous birth complication that contributes to around 29% of maternal deaths in his home country, Tanzania. The Mkanda Salama (“Safe Wrap” in Swahili) is easy to use and costs just $70. A study found that it stopped postpartum bleeding in 73% of women within 20 minutes.
2. Making brain electrodes inspired by Japanese art
For decades, scientists have been developing, testing, and implanting brain electrodes. These devices are literally inserted into people’s brains, so while they can help us understand brain activity and treat various neurological disorders, it’s not totally surprising that they can also cause a bit of damage. Xiao Yang, 34, is working on ultra-small electrodes, which she hopes will have less of an impact on surrounding brain tissue. Her electrodes are flexible, too—in fact, they look a lot like actual neurons.
Yang is also creating sheets of electrodes to study brain cells in the lab. Inspired by kirigami—the traditional Japanese art of cutting paper to form three-dimensional shapes—she’s created a sheet of electrodes with a honeycombed structure shaped like a spiral basket. And she’s already using it to study brain cells.
3. Developing an all-new treatment for baby KJ
In 2024, Kyle “KJ” Muldoon Jr. was born with a rare and potentially fatal genetic disorder. Sarah Grandinette was a member of a team that developed an entirely new, personalized treatment for him—a gene-editing therapy essentially designed to correct a genetic misspelling.
Grandinette, who is now 26, created cells with KJ’s genetic variant and used them to screen gene-editing approaches; then she tested potential medicines in mice and monkeys. KJ ultimately got his first dose of the resulting treatment when he was about seven months old. He responded well and was eventually discharged from hospital. He’s “doing pretty great,” she says.
4. Reversing the aging process to treat eye disease
The buzziest tech in longevity right now centers on reprogramming—attempts to rewind the age of cells by resetting them to a more embryonic-like state. In a study published in 2020, Yuancheng (Ryan) Lu (now 34) and his colleagues showed that a reprogramming therapy reversed vision loss in aged, blind mice. Now an almost identical version of that therapy is being tested in people with eye disease. Life Biosciences, the company developing the drug, dosed its first volunteer in June.
5. Using AI to design new viruses
Last year, Samuel King used a generative AI model to come up with new genetic blueprints for bacteriophages—teeny viruses that can infect bacteria. Once he had those blueprints, he printed them out as strands of DNA. In experiments, he found that those AI-designed viruses could create new copies of themselves, burst out of bacterial cells, and infect other nearby bacteria. Viruses aren’t alive, but King, 27, hopes that AI-designed life forms might one day be used to make drugs or soak up pollution.

- [iPhone 18 和 Duo 发布会上，Apple 没告诉你的那些事](https://sspai.com/post/114449) <sub>09-11 16:30</sub>

<summary>📄 展开阅读全文（6000 字）</summary>

又是 9 月，又是一年一度熬夜看发布会的日子。然而今年的开场却有「亿点」不一样：当视频的灯光打向我们熟悉的 Tim Cook 的时候，他摆了摆手说「不是我，是这位」，然后镜头往前一推，John Ternus 走上台，说出了他作为新 CEO 主持发布会的第一句问候：「Hello」。
这场名为「Surprise and Shine」的发布会，看点相比往年也要密集许多。
其中，这次的主角毫无疑问是 Apple 的第一台折叠屏手机 iPhone Duo，其它产品如 iPhone 18 Pro、Apple Watch 和 AirPods 5 也有不小的升级。但仅仅只有一个半小时的 Keynote，总还是让人觉得意犹未尽。因此和往年一样，我们在发布会后火速把各个渠道的内容又翻了一遍，整理了 Apple 在发布会上没有告诉你的那些事。
关于 iPhone Duo 的那些事
Apple 推出的第一款折叠屏设备得名 iPhone Duo 而不是传言很久的 iPhone Ultra，说明 Apple 对它的定位其实并不是「比 Pro 更高」。相反，它的定位或许更像 iPhone Air，即算是一个新的系列，保留 iPhone 和折叠屏幕的核心体验，并在某些地方做到极致，而影像、峰值性能等一些地方相比 Pro 则有所妥协甚至缩水。
下面让我们看看如果今年打算买 iPhone Duo 的话，会比 iPhone 18 Pro 少了哪些功能。
延时摄影视频没缩水
在官网规格页上，iPhone Duo 的视频拍摄里写着「延时摄影视频，支持防抖功能（最高可达 4K 杜比视界）」。这是 iPhone Duo 唯一一个与 Pro 影像功能平起平坐的地方了。
Duo 了个屏幕，少了些功能
除了上面一条之外，Apple 对 iPhone Duo 的影像系统直接来了个大砍刀，刘华强看了都觉得残忍那种。我们把 iPhone 18 Pro、iPhone Duo 和 iPhone 17 的对比放一块，你就知道它和谁坐一桌了。
换句话说，iPhone Duo 是一台价位接近旗舰，不过影像规格接近标准版、iPhone Air 的设备。
噢，差点忘了。iPhone Duo 也没有操作按钮，但我觉得 Apple 下一代会给 iPhone Duo 加上，并当一个新的卖点。
史上最薄也最重的 iPhone
iPhone Duo 在展开状态下的厚度为 5.2 毫米，相比 iPhone Air 的 5.64 毫米还要再薄 0.4 毫米，是 Apple 有史以来最薄的 iPhone。但这台最薄的 iPhone，也是有史以来 Apple 最重的 iPhone：254 克，超过了半斤，比 iPhone 18 Pro Max 还要重 5 克。
或许 5 克对很多人来说不算大，但对我这种 iPhone Air 用户来说就很夸张了：iPhone Duo 比 iPhone Air 重了 89 克，差不多是 4 节半 5 号电池。
展开之后，充电口在左边
目前市售的大部分折叠屏手机，充电口普遍放在展开后的右侧，但 iPhone Duo 则反过来，把接口在左侧。或许是 Apple 考虑到 Dock、按钮和扬声器放在了右边，再放一个充电口就很拥挤了。
当然这也带来一个好处和坏处。好处是折叠起来之后，USB-C 接口位于上方，比较厚的 USB-C 充电线也能怼得进去；坏处是每次你展开屏幕，线就得跟着跑个半圆。
顺带一提，iPhone Duo 的接口和 Pro 又是一样的 USB 3 10Gb/s，还支持 DisplayPort 输出 4K HDR。
营业厅应该从没有这么 Duo 人来办过 eSIM 卡
由于 iPhone Duo 过于轻薄，因此它和 iPhone Air 一样也只支持 eSIM。
所以不管是一年一换闭眼就买的发烧友，还是为了第一时间尝鲜新形态的追新族，又或者是各类为了评测、体验入手的数码博主，只要是国行 iPhone Duo 用户，大家都会在当地的运营商营业厅里相遇。
很难讲这次的 iPhone Duo 会不会是国内一次 eSIM 的普及风暴。
Duo 了块电池，但充电功率和 18 Pro 略有不同
iPhone 18 Pro 的充电功率再次得到提升，最高很可能可以跑到 60W，15 分钟就能充 50% 的电量，基本赶上了如今国产 Android 设备的入门水平。但翻看下面的小字发现，想要达到这个速度，必须得用支持可调电压供电（AVS）的 60 瓦或更大功率充电头，所以又找到一个买新充电头的理由啦。
相比之下，iPhone Duo 大概是 iPhone 17 系列的水平，20 分钟能充 50% 左右的电量。但与 17 系列只要 40W 以上充电头不同的是，iPhone Duo 也得要 60W 以上的充电头才行，但不用支持 AVS。不过换个角度看，iPhone Duo 里面装着两块电池，充电时两块电池一起充电，20 分钟能充到 50%，其实速度不算差了。
至少比起 iPhone Air 这个 30 分钟只能充 50% 的弟中弟来说，已经算是飞跃了。
没为 Duo 更新的 App，会怎么显示
Android 设备做折叠屏这么多年来，应用适配的情况一直不容乐观。因此你或许也会好奇，那些没给 iPhone Duo 适配的 App 会怎么显示？
Apple 在发布会后更新给开发者的技术讲座视频里给出了答案：没有为 iPhone Duo 更新的 App，Apple 设计了三种兼容模式来过渡。一部分使用旧 SDK，还没适配 iOS 27 的 App，会被限制在一块安全区域内、周围留着黑边。而适配了 iOS 27 的 App，会自动适配大部分的屏幕面积，但右侧的状态栏边缘仍会留白。
只有使用了 iOS 27.1 SDK 开发的 App，才能真正铺满内屏。

Apple Watch 采集到的原始音频会直接进入 S11 芯片内部的安全缓冲区，并经过加密发送到配对 iPhone 的 Secure Exclave。数据来到 iPhone 上以后，会先进行语音转写和语义压缩。如果需要最终总结，才会发送到云端 Private Cloud Compute 处理。
Apple 认为，这个功能不是录音，因为在 Apple Watch 上采集并处理后的音频会被立即删除；而且用户也可以随时控制这个功能在什么情况下才打开。至于 Siri Recap 生成的摘要，如果用户没有保存或导出，会在 7 天后自动删除；Live Rewind 的文字片段则会在屏幕熄灭约 30 秒后消失。

预算
我该花多少钱买车？
想必这是每一个计划购车的人首先要面对的问题。
近几年的经济形势的确并不算乐观，但就我个人而言，目前我仍处于一个相对稳定的职业发展阶段：有着较为稳定的工作，父母也都还在工作，短期内没有结婚等大额支出的压力。因此我最终决定以自己一年的到手收入作为购车预算。
现在回过头来看，我觉得这或许也是一个比较合适预算标准——既不会因为买车而给未来的生活带来过重负担，也能够在能力范围内选择一款真正让自己满意的产品。

空间表现

            Abstract:World Action Models (WAMs) couple predictive world modeling with action generation, allowing anticipated future states to guide agent behavior. NSF moves to overhaul funding approach in line with White House priorities
The major funder of basic science has not said how its new initiatives will be funded — but staff worry streamlined approach will further squeeze cash for core sciences.
The US National Science Foundation (NSF) is changing how it funds research grants — and how it decides which ones to fund — to better align with White House priorities, according to a letter released on 10 September.
“NSF is taking decisive action to meet this moment, introducing new and ambitious ideas that capitalize on the changes in the scientific ecosystem to advance our core mission,” says the letter. It is signed by Brian Stone, who is standing in as the NSF’s director.
Since its inception in 1950, the NSF, a major funder of basic research, has solicited proposals on topics informed heavily by the scientific community. The letter describes a shift to a “portfolio-based” approach to making grants that combines traditional “investigator-driven inquiry” with “NSF-directed opportunities”. The letter does not say what the opportunities will be, but states that the NSF will specify objectives but “never the method scientists must use” to achieve them.
Staff members at the agency, who spoke on the condition of anonymity out of fear of retaliation, are concerned that the changes will lead to further cuts for work that has long been core to the agency’s mission: discovery-focused research, led by the curiosity of scientists.
When asked for comment on criticisms of the changes, an NSF spokesperson directed Nature to Stone’s letter.
The letter says that solicitations for grant proposals will be “consolidated” and “simplified”. Calls for proposals will also be designed with a greater focus on advancing “a specific aspect of NSF’s mission”, the letter adds.
Central to the NSF’s portfolio-based approach are four research topics that will receive funding from across the agency’s directorates and will also require “cooperation across disciplines and directorates” the letter says. It names four “Frontier Initiatives”: the Future of Intelligence, Frontier of Quantum Phenomena, Advanced Materials and Matter, and Foundations of Biological Design.
These initiatives are a brainchild of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), according to earlier reporting by Nature. The letter does not specify how much will be spent on the frontier initiatives, or how they will be funded, but it says that they will “make full use of the agency’s funding mechanisms … including prizes and challenges”. NSF staff have told Nature that some of the money is likely to come out of the US$1 billion in funding that the agency has been holding in a central account instead of allocating it to grants.
Staff members say that the move to fund the frontier initiatives will probably shrink the agency’s core-science funding and divert large sums to White House priorities. “Ordinarily, there would be optimism that these initiatives could further strengthen the agency’s mission,” one staff member says. “The current administration, however, seems intent on undermining science, so naturally there is scepticism about all of this.”

At a time when misinformation is rife and many seem to be questioning scientific evidence, do people really trust scientists? In the United Kingdom, the answer is mostly yes, finds a study led by the Policy Institute at King’s College London and Sense about Science, a charity based in London.
Nearly two-thirds (63%) of 4,262 UK adults surveyed said they trust scientists, giving a score of 7 or higher on a zero to ten scale. Only 5% rated their trust at three or below.
To explore what drives trust and distrust of scientists, the researchers used regression modelling to identify factors most strongly associated with trust. The most influential factors were whether respondents felt that scientists are "on the side of people like them" and whether they thought of scientists as open and transparent. Both outweighed perceptions of scientific expertise.
Previous research has shown that benevolence — the idea that researchers care about society and want to do good — is a core pillar of public trust in science1. Now Bobby Duffy, director of the Policy Institute at King’s College London and one of the authors of the UK study, says that the work reveals that trust in scientists was less associated with scientists being eager to work for the good of society in general (12%) and had more to do with whether they are on the side of people like the respondent (21%).
“In a politically divided society, people become much more interested in, ‘What’s in it for me?’” says Duffy.
Where trust falters
When the researchers delved into specific fields, they found that trust declined for scientists working on vaccines, space and climate (see ‘Pockets of trust’). And more people had more confidence in academics (12%) rather than in scientists working in government (42%) and in private companies (34%). Perhaps unsurprisingly, people with little trust in scientists are more likely to think conspiracy theories about climate change, vaccines and the effects of 5G phone towers are true, says the report.
