Cindy is an open-source AI agent that works out of the box. She brings multiple harnesses, models and tools into one agent that finishes real work in your projects and apps. Ready from day one, yours to shape over time.
Cindy runs locally on your own machine, using your real files and logged-in apps. The first supported harnesses are Claude Code and Codex — more are being added, and a native harness is in the works. Models and harnesses mix freely and can switch mid-task while your workspace, memory, skills and tools stay continuous; one task can even be planned, executed in parallel, and reviewed by agents on different harness × model combos. She can drive your browser, computer and phone, and take work from IM and schedules.
This repository is the open-source client for Cindy — the desktop and mobile apps plus their shared packages, organized as a pnpm monorepo.
The client is free to use, and its source code is open under Apache-2.0. Bring models your way: sign in to the official Cindy service (usage deducted transparently), authorize the Claude Code / Codex Coding Plan you already pay for and keep using it inside Cindy — no duplicate bill — connect your own API keys, or use local models. Linux users: see the Ubuntu, Arch Linux and Omarchy installation guide for installation, updates, keyring setup and migration from a manual install.

A design skill for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and OpenCode that refuses to look AI-generated.
usehallmark.com · forty-eight themes · custom on request · redesign and audit by name
Made by Together AI.
Hallmark holds a hard floor and then gets out of the way. It deals three themes from a catalog of forty-eight materials, builds the page around what the visitor came to do, and lints the result in a real browser against the tells that read as machine-made: the eyebrow above every heading, tracked-capital labels, one coloured word in a headline, a page of empty frames or nothing but photographs, a hero that misses its screen, an email sign-up at the end of every page, a form that says thanks without sending anything. A fresh-context reviewer gets the last word.
The gates come in tiers. The Floor never ships (contrast, focus, reduced motion, invented facts, a broken phone layout). Reflex gates are the defaults a model falls into; a build with a reason may overrule one on the record. Finish checks are craft detail that never blocks a ship.
- Forty-eight themes. Each is a material (paper, type, colour, surfaces, its own components and texture) with a picture of a finished page, and the build lays out its own page in it.
- Built from the job. A booking page books, a shop sells, a newsletter subscribes: the action sits where the category puts it, and the page ends on it.
- Custom. Ask for a custom page and Hallmark designs from your prompt alone, under the same rules and checks.
- Your real product. Point it at a URL, a dev server or an app and it captures the screenshots itself.
- Real photographs. 755 openly licensed photos, picked per subject and credited on the page.
- Redesign and audit. /hallmark redesign <path> rebuilds a page and keeps its content and wiring; /hallmark audit <path> only reports.
Ten of the forty-eight. A theme is a material, not a plan: it never says what a page contains or in what order, so two prompts in one theme make two different pages. Each is one file of at most 8 KB in references/themes/ with a picture beside it; a build reads exactly one. See them all at usehallmark.com.
Say custom and Hallmark drops the themes: no plan, no components, no palette recipe, only the floor, the gates and a short direction in direction.md to find one idea that only this client has and let it run the page.
- Pre-flight reads the project (fonts, palette, framework, a locked design.md) and asks once for the material: photos, screenshots, real numbers. Nothing is invented.
- scripts/theme.mjs deal offers three themes that fit the prompt and clear the log; draw deals the page's anatomy from its job.
- The hero is built alone, rendered and reviewed cold before the rest of the page exists.
- scripts/sloplint.mjs --render checks the page at eight widths, then a subagent reviews it with fresh eyes.
Each page is self-contained HTML and CSS, stamped in its first CSS comment with the decisions it took.
Hallmark never starts on a design that already exists unless you name it:
/hallmark redesign ./src/app
/hallmark audit ./index.html
A full redesign keeps the content and the wiring and changes the system and the layout; say keep the layout for a restyle or polish for only the tells. sloplint --before records every link, field, handler and heading first, sloplint --kept fails the redesign if one went missing, and the handoff is a side-by-side before and after.
npx skills add nutlope/hallmark -g
-g installs for every project; leave it off to install into the current one. Needs Node 18 or later. The browser checks (sloplint --render, screenshots) also need npm i -g puppeteer-core and Chrome; without them the static checks still run.
- One harness only: npx skills add nutlope/hallmark -g --agent claude-code (or codex, opencode, cursor, github-copilot).
- As a Claude Code plugin: /plugin marketplace add nutlope/hallmark, then /plugin install hallmark@hallmark (invoked as /hallmark:hallmark).
- From a clone: node skills/hallmark/scripts/install.mjs validates every theme and reference before it copies; install-hook.mjs adds an edit-time Floor check to Claude Code.
- Lint any page: node skills/hallmark/scripts/sloplint.mjs <file-or-dir> --render.
- If it does not fire on its own, name it: use hallmark to build ....
MIT. Use it, fork it, ship it.
The photo library (skills/hallmark/scripts/photos.json) lists photographs hosted on Wikimedia Commons and holds no image files. A tiny biped robot that moves using reinforcement learning policies.

This repo is the duck's brain. About 25 cm and 800 g of robot, run by a handful of daemons on a Rockchip RK3566: a 50 Hz control loop driving fifteen servos from neural policies, the radios and the camera, and the update machinery that gets new software onto a robot without bricking it.
Everything you need to run a Microduck is here. If you want one, get yours here.
The policies it runs are trained next door, in microduck_rl — MuJoCo and PPO, the sim2real recipe, and the export to ONNX that this repo loads.
It also sits, kicks a ball, rolls forward on command, and quacks in a voice that is its own.
Rust, no framework, one workspace. robotd owns the control loop and the motor bus; updaterd
installs signed releases and rolls them back when a robot comes up unhealthy; configd owns wifi
and identity; btd is the Bluetooth path a phone uses; padd reads the gamepad; mediad streams
the camera over WebRTC; tofd serves the depth sensor. They talk over one JSON-RPC contract on
Unix sockets, and every client — the app, the console, the gamepad, your script — sends exactly the
same calls.
The interesting decisions are written down: docs/design/ is why things are the
way they are, and docs/project/ is what has gone wrong and what would close it.
No duck was harmed in the making of this robot. Several were consulted.

全面护理

我在之前的文章中就有讲过，比起叠加层层功效提升皮肤的「能力」，保持皮肤自身状态的稳定更重要。为了保持皮肤稳定，我们可以在强效精华和强效面霜之间选择一个作为护肤的主力军，另一个可以直接省略。举例来说，你选择了一款抗氧化补水补油都很不错的精华油，就不用再叠加一个什么都能干的强效面霜了，来一个普通的保湿面霜或者乳液就已经足够。或者你手里已经有一个全能面霜，那前面就不需要用一个强效的精华液或精华油了，直接用一个精华水就可以。
不过，护肤搭配的老手可以在这个方法上更进一步：搭配活性物种类不同的精华和面霜。具体来说，一个是要避免选择功效性相同的多种活性物，另一个是把活性物充分组合力求覆盖护肤金字塔的更多面。这个方法非常依赖于你个人的使用经验以及皮肤的稳定情况，并不是按照护肤金字塔把活性物凑齐了就算完事儿。具体操作的时候，建议你先把手里的产品成分按照护肤金字塔分分类，然后在覆盖面最大化的原则下自由组合，看哪个用着效果最稳定就选择哪种。
精华水推荐
再次强调，精华水只适合一般、混合和油性肤质，干性肤质在秋冬不建议使用，敏感肌也最好选择合适的精华液或精华油。并且，由于我们的皮肤天然亲脂，而精华水又很明显是水向质地，所以并不是十分利于很多活性物的发挥。这也就意味着，不要对精华水的抗氧化和抗糖化等功效抱有很强的期待，在秋冬季节，它们更适合作为一个功效性乳液或者面霜之前的打底。
先推荐一款相对万能的精华水——三式解忧精粹水。解忧精粹水和我之前推荐的宝塔眼霜和宝塔面霜属于同一个系列，只是适用范围更广一些，几乎全肤质适用。这支精华水的核心成分是 3% 的传明酸和 2% 的烟酰胺，主要负责提亮和细嫩皮肤，同时复配的依克多因和白芍药花提取物能平衡刺激性，让产品使用起来更温和。在我看来，作为一支精华水，这款产品的功效覆盖全面，也适合各类肤质的人群使用，是一款合格的万用产品。大促期间，三式解忧精粹水的价格大概是 200-300 元之间，受不同优惠力度的影响价格可能有所波动。

Matrix 是少数派的写作社区，我们主张分享真实的产品体验，有实用价值的经验与思考。我们会不定期挑选 Matrix 最优质的文章，展示来自用户的最真实的体验和观点。

大家好，我是飘雷，今天为大家带来的是 Intel 傲腾 P5800X 1.6TB 的深入测评报告。
在固态硬盘演进史中最负盛名的 SCM 产品，Intel 和 Micron 合作研发的傲腾（Optane）系列无疑是极为特殊的存在。
它并非基于我们熟知的 NAND 闪存技术，而是采用了 Intel 与美光联合研发的 3D XPoint 存储介质，在底层原理就与最常见的 NAND 闪存截然不同。
NAND 颗粒依靠向浮栅晶体管或者电荷阱注入电子来存储数据，通过不同电压来标识数据状态；而且 NAND 闪存以 Page 为读写单位，但是以多个 Page 组成的 Block 为擦除单位，这就导致其在写入数据之前，如果目标 Block 中已有旧数据，需要先对整个区块执行擦除操作。
而 3D XPoint 则利用相变存储（融化/结晶）原理，通过改变硫系化合物材料的电阻状态来标识 0 和 1，并且实现了按字节寻址的特性，写入时可以直接覆盖改写，而无需先擦后写。
正是存储介质在物理特性上的根本差异，使得傲腾系列 SSD 拥有极低的读写延迟、超高且稳定的随机读写性能，以及堪称变态的写入寿命，至今令几乎所有 NAND SSD 望尘莫及。
可惜的是，傲腾这颗存储界的明珠并没有在商业上获得应有的成功。
由于 3D XPoint 颗粒成本高昂，傲腾产品的定价一直居高不下，曲高和寡的结局便是销量始终未能打开局面，Intel 最终在 2022 年第二季度财报中正式宣布逐步关停傲腾业务，在美光将其位于犹他州 Lehi、唯一量产 3D XPoint 的晶圆厂出售给德州仪器后，整条傲腾产品线已成绝响。
傲腾系列固态硬盘最终没有迈入 PCIe 5.0 时代，停留在 PCIe 4.0 的产品线主要有 P5800X、P5801X、P5810X 和神秘的 P5820X。
有种说法是，P5800X 和 P5801X 使用的是第二代的 3D XPoint 颗粒，而 P5810X 和 P5820X 又用回了第一代 3D XPoint 颗粒，在同容量的情况下，die 数量翻倍，导致功耗有所提高，性能反而不如 P5800X。
所以个人觉得，PCIe 4.0 傲腾中最吸引人的，当属 1.6TB 和 3.2TB 容量的满血 P5800X。
以 Intel 傲腾 P5800X 1.6TB 为例，这款产品于 2020 年第 4 季度发布，标称顺序读取可达 7200 MB/s，顺序写入6200 MB/s，4K随机读写性能均超过1500K IOPS，且 4K 随机读写延迟在 6μs 以下，即便放在今天，随机读写性能和延迟表现依然是业内天顶星水平。
更夸张的是，Intel 傲腾 P5800X 1.6TB 的标称终身写入量高达 100 DWPD，也即在 5 年质保周期内每天都支持写入 100 倍全盘容量的数据，换算过来为 292 PB（292000 TB），是市售 2TB 消费级 SSD（1200 TBW）的 240 多倍！
更恐怖的是，3D XPoint 颗粒的实际写入寿命还要远超与此，至今几乎没有人能真正测出极限。
比如贴吧老哥 @CinemaDNG 对 16GB 的傲腾 M10 进行了 3 年多的持续写入测试，全盘已经累计写入近 30 万次，依然活蹦乱跳。
傲腾系列 SSD 如外星科技一样的性能和寿命表现，让它成为众多 SSD 爱好者心中的白月光和朱砂痣，只可惜此前它的售价极为昂贵，例如傲腾 P5800X 1.6TB 的全新国行价格要在 3 万元左右，小黄鱼二手的价格也要超过 1 万元。
不过好消息是，前段时间小黄鱼上突然出现了一批傲腾 P5800X 的大船货源，400GB 版本的成交价大约在 1000 元出头，而 1.6TB 的满血版本则在 4200 元左右。

iFixit 拆解 iPhone Duo，肯定可维修性但指出内屏隐患
10 月 25 日，iFixit 发布了 iPhone Duo 的拆解报告。该机获得了 5 分（满分 10 分）的可修复性评分（后续可能修订），为目前获评最高的折叠屏智能手机。但报告亦指出，其内屏与铰链仍存在明显的设计隐患与高昂的维修门槛。
iPhone Duo 之所以获得较高的可修复评分，主要得益于其扁平化的内部拆解结构。该机采用单层主板设计，外屏与边框可完整无损拆下；双电池均采用电释放粘合剂与金属外壳，降低了拆卸难度与刺穿风险。不过，整机拆解仍需使用五种不同规格的螺丝刀，且苹果官方自助维修配件尚未正式上架。
报告指出，目前已有多名用户在开合手机时听到异响，随后内屏折痕处出现裂纹及亮线，部分故障甚至发生在首次开机阶段。iFixit 通过 3D 建模与工业 CT 扫描分析，认为故障点集中在跨屏排线缺口与铰链中央支撑结构的交界处。若中央支撑杆在闭合时未能充分下沉，屏幕折叠处便会受到挤压并产生应力集中。此外，防尘测试显示细小粉尘仍会进入铰链内部并导致开合异响。
在显示结构上，苹果通过水滴形铰链、多达 32 种位移的椭圆形子像素排列，以及可更换的哑光纳米纹理聚合物表层来散射光线，从而在视觉上隐藏折痕。若剥离该表层，其折痕明显程度与三星 Galaxy Z Fold8 相当。

Title:Tradeoff-Aware Territory Design: A Multi-Objective Decision System for Enterprise Customer-to-Seller Allocation
View PDF HTML (experimental)
            Abstract:Enterprise customer journeys are shaped not only by product experiences and marketing, but also by recurring resource-allocation decisions. In B2B go-to-market (GTM) organizations, territory carving determines which accounts receive seller coverage, which seller-account relationships are preserved, and how limited field capacity is distributed. Yet the process often remains manual, making it difficult to compare opportunities, value continuity, and evaluate tradeoffs among growth, balance, capacity, and feasibility.
We present a production decision system for tradeoff-aware customer-to-seller allocation in enterprise GTM planning. It integrates three components: a multidimensional account-value layer that combines predictive signals to score opportunity; an observational continuity-valuation layer that estimates the cost of breaking seller-account relationships using a synthetic-control-style counterfactual framework; and a multi-objective optimization layer that allocates coverage to high-value accounts while considering continuity, opportunity balance, and operational constraints.
Deployed as a scenario-based decision-support system, the framework enables planners to inspect and compare allocation tradeoffs. Historical backtesting shows that optimizer-generated scenarios substantially improve assignment compliance while maintaining comparable seller-level opportunity distribution. In production planning, adopted scenarios improved both assignment compliance and opportunity balance relative to the previous baseline. Directing scarce seller capacity toward higher-value accounts also offers incremental revenue potential at enterprise scale. The key contribution is an integrated, inspectable framework that turns territory carving into a governable decision process.

The Revolutionary Origins of LanguageChris Knight & Jerome Lewis Yale Univ. Press (2026)
The origins of writing and script remain unclear, but those of speech and language are simply unknown. This is because no physical evidence remains of the earliest language, which was probably spoken in Africa, the birthplace of Homo sapiens. By contrast, inscribed marks on clay tablets that constitute the earliest known form of writing — however mysterious — have survived from ancient Mesopotamia, dating to the fourth millennium bc.
That’s left a lot of room for scholars to speculate on how speech and language began, starting with thinkers from the ancient world, including Plato, who could not decide whether language had a natural or a purely human origin. By the nineteenth century, anthropologists had grown tired of so much talk with scant evidence to go on.
In 1866, the founding statutes of the Paris Linguistic Society forbade all discussion of the subject, note anthropologists Chris Knight and Jerome Lewis in The Revolutionary Origins of Language. “Anyone theorizing about the origins of language risked being dismissed as a crank.” Even so, “from time to time, this or that brave maverick would risk whatever reputation they had by attempting to reopen the debate”.
Science of linguistics
The best known, if controversial, attempts since then come from linguist Noam Chomsky, starting in the 1960s. The authors discuss his contributions at length, drawing on Knight’s book Decoding Chomsky (2016).
In 1987, Chomsky remarked in Language and Problems of Knowledge that: “There is a long history of study of the origin of language, asking how it came from the calls of apes and so forth.” But, in his view, this investigation is a “complete waste of time”.
That’s because language is based on “an entirely different principle than any animal communication system”, he wrote. Although human gestures might have evolved from animal communication systems, the same could not apply to human language, Chomsky thought.
This view was supported by an academic study, launched in 1973, of a young chimpanzee (Pan troglodytes) with the punning name Nim Chimpsky. Nim learnt from his human supervisors how to make dozens of hand signs to procure food and other rewards, but not how to communicate in American Sign Language or to speak a language. “Give orange me give eat orange me eat orange give me eat orange give me you” was Nim’s longest recorded sign-language sequence, as cited by Knight and Lewis.
In Chomsky’s view, human language arose suddenly because of a single genetic mutation, not from Darwinian natural selection or through a gradual, adaptive cultural process. In other words, argue the authors, by declaring language to be “an especially intriguing and mysterious aspect of the natural world, to be investigated using the normal methods of natural science”, Chomsky elevated the study of linguistics to a scientific challenge in the field of anthropology.
However, they add, “there is no measuring device, magnifying glass, telescope or other instrument which can grasp the meaning of a word.” So how can conventional science investigate language?
Social origins
Rejecting Chomsky’s view, Knight and Lewis instead offer a complex mixture of evidence, both scientific and cultural, to support their own theory. This evidence is mostly drawn from prehistoric and present-day Africa, where Lewis spent three decades studying hunter-gatherers, notably the BaYaka people in Central Africa.
“To qualify as science, a hypothesis must suggest testable predictions,” the authors remark. Given that there can be no direct evidence for events in the distant past, scientists must therefore “construct tests from a wide range of telltale clues”. These include: “recurrent structures of magical myth, initiation rituals, hunters’ taboos, patterns of menstrual prohibition, lunar and solar influences, and critically, archaeological evidence for symbolism in the form of red ochre pigments and personal ornaments”. All of these clues are examined in their intriguing, if rambling, book.
Although anthropologists have often suggested that speech and language arose from hunting or warfare, Knight and Lewis refute these two theories. While hunting in the forest, male hunter-gatherers “do their utmost to avoid having to speak aloud”, so as to lure animals towards them. Instead, they use sign language to alert each other to the nearby presence of animals such as gorillas, antelopes, leopards and elephants.
